A student-led sustainability organization where I work with others to turn environmental awareness into everyday action.

Vice President
Why ECOBO

Environmental awareness only matters when it becomes behavior.

ECOBO is a student-led sustainability organization focused on building a greener and more environmentally conscious school culture.

Our work combines environmental education, recycling, student participation, and systems that encourage everyday responsibility.

What makes ECOBO meaningful to me is that it is not only about talking about environmental problems. It is about creating structures that help people do something about them.

My Role

Leadership means building a system others can grow in.

As Vice President, I work with a team of 60+ volunteers to coordinate sustainability projects across the school.

My role involves organizing people, planning initiatives, communicating ideas, and helping turn individual enthusiasm into consistent school-wide action.

I learned that leadership is not about doing everything yourself. It is about creating a structure that allows other people to contribute.

What We Built

Turning environmental values into systems.

ECOBO's work is not limited to events. We created recurring systems that make sustainability part of everyday school life.

01 · EDUCATION

Environmental Seminars

Organized regular seminars to raise awareness around conservation, recycling, biodiversity, and environmental responsibility.

02 · RECYCLING

Waste Management

Collected, sorted, and coordinated the delivery of recyclable materials from across the school to official recycling centers.

03 · PARTICIPATION

Classroom Evaluation

Implemented daily classroom evaluations using defined cleanliness criteria to encourage consistent participation.

04 · MOTIVATION

Recognition System

Introduced weekly and monthly classroom recognition through specially designed flags, making progress visible and rewarding.

A Defining Moment

Bringing environmental science into the school.

ECOBO had the opportunity to host Prof. Dr. Latif Kurt, a botanist and environmental scientist, for a special seminar attended by more than 900 students.


The event focused on biodiversity, conservation, and the importance of protecting our natural heritage. For me, it was an example of how a student-led initiative can create a bridge between academic expertise and a wider school community.

900+ STUDENTS ATTENDED
What I Learned

People are the most important part of impact.

ECOBO changed the way I think about environmental action. A good idea is not enough. It needs people who understand it, a structure that makes participation possible, and a reason to keep going. Leading volunteers taught me to listen, delegate, communicate clearly, and think about sustainability not only as an environmental problem, but also as a human one.
