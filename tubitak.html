TÜBİTAK 4006 · Research

Alternative Protein

Investigating Tenebrio molitor larvae as a potential sustainable protein source for a changing food system.

A research project combining laboratory observation, literature review, cultivation, and sustainability analysis.

Food Security Sustainability Research Laboratory
The Question

Can insects become part of a more sustainable food system?

Global population growth, climate change, water scarcity, and shrinking agricultural resources raise an important question: how can future food systems provide enough protein while using fewer resources?

Our project investigated whether Tenebrio molitor larvae could represent one possible answer.

Research focus: Can mealworm larvae be cultivated effectively and offer meaningful nutritional and environmental advantages as an alternative protein source?

Methodology

From cultivation to analysis.

We combined practical laboratory work with background research to evaluate both cultivation and the potential sustainability of mealworms.

01 · LIFE CYCLE

Life Cycle Analysis

We studied the development of mealworms through the stages of egg, larva, pupa, and adult beetle.

02 · CULTIVATION

Laboratory Farming

Mealworms were raised under laboratory conditions to observe their development and cultivation process.

03 · FEEDING

Feeding Regimen

Larvae were fed wheat bran and organic materials, including carrots, as part of the cultivation process.

04 · LITERATURE

Literature Review

Scientific literature was reviewed to understand nutritional value, cultivation practices, and sustainability considerations.

05 · OBSERVATION

Observation & Analysis

Observations and comparative analysis were used to evaluate the results of the cultivation process.

06 · DOCUMENTATION

Experimental Record

The process was documented through observations, experimental data, and photographic records.

Findings

What the research suggested.

The project pointed toward several potential advantages of mealworm cultivation while also highlighting the need for further research.

High Protein Content

Literature reviewed for the project reports protein levels of up to approximately 50% in mealworm larvae.

Low Resource Requirements

Mealworms can be cultivated using relatively little water and space and have a comparatively short development cycle.

Organic Waste Utilization

The cultivation process can make use of organic materials as part of the feeding regimen.

Potential Environmental Benefit

The literature reviewed suggests potential environmental advantages compared with conventional livestock systems.

Successful Cultivation

Larval and adult stages were successfully obtained during the laboratory cultivation process.

Further Questions

Cultural acceptance, legal frameworks, food hygiene, and large-scale implementation remain important considerations.

Conclusion

A small organism raised a much bigger question.

Our findings suggest that Tenebrio molitor has potential as an alternative protein source because of its nutritional value, cultivation requirements, and ability to develop under controlled conditions.

At the same time, the project showed that sustainability is more complicated than a single metric. Nutrition, resource use, environmental impact, regulation, hygiene, and social acceptance all have to be considered together.

Project perspective: Rather than treating insect farming as a complete solution, we approached it as one possible direction for future food-system research.
What I Learned

Research changed the way I think about sustainability.

This project taught me that sustainability is not simply about finding something that looks environmentally friendly. It requires asking questions, examining evidence, testing ideas, and being willing to discover limitations. Working with a real organism also made the research process much more concrete: we had to observe what happened rather than only discuss what might happen.
