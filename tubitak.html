TÜBİTAK 4006

Research · Sustainable Protein

About the Project

Alternatif Protein Kaynağı: Böcek Yetiştiriciliği ve Besin Değeri Analizi (Alternative Protein Source: Insect Farming and Nutritional Value Analysis) is a TÜBİTAK 4006 research project exploring the potential of Tenebrio molitor (mealworm) larvae as a sustainable protein source for the future.

🧬 Project Information
Project Type: Research
Main Field: Food and Food Supply Security
Thematic Topic: Food and Food Supply Security
Advisor Teacher: Nazlı ÇOBAN YEŞİL

👥 Project Team

Azra Gül BAKIR Elifsu ARİFOĞLU Meryem ÖZER

❓ Problem Statement

The world population is growing rapidly, but food production is not keeping pace. According to Malthus's theory, population grows geometrically while food production grows arithmetically. Climate change, water scarcity, and decreasing agricultural land create a risk of protein deficiency in the future. This situation makes alternative and sustainable protein sources essential.

🔬 Methodology

  • 🔄
    Life Cycle Analysis: The life cycle of mealworms (Egg → Larva → Pupa → Adult Beetle) was studied.
  • 🧪
    Laboratory Farming: Mealworms were raised in laboratory conditions.
  • 🌾
    Feeding Regimen: Larvae were fed wheat bran and organic waste (carrots, etc.).
  • 📚
    Literature Review: Nutritional values were researched through scientific literature.
  • 📊
    Observation & Analysis: Comparative analysis and observations were conducted.

📊 Findings

  • 🔹 High Protein Content: Mealworm larvae contain up to 50% protein.
  • 🔹 Low Resource Requirements: Requires very little water and space, reproduces quickly.
  • 🔹 Organic Waste Utilization: Can be easily fed with organic waste.
  • 🔹 Environmental Friendliness: Lower carbon emissions compared to traditional livestock farming.
  • 🔹 Successful Cultivation: Larval and adult stages were successfully obtained in laboratory conditions.

📝 Conclusion & Discussion

Tenebrio molitor larvae have great potential as a sustainable, economical, and environmentally friendly alternative protein source for the growing world population and increasing protein needs.

With high protein content (up to 50%), low water and space requirements, ability to feed on organic waste, and low carbon footprint, they offer significant advantages over traditional livestock. In laboratory experiments, larval and adult stages were successfully obtained, demonstrating that farming is practically feasible.

While there are challenges such as cultural prejudices, legal regulations, and hygiene standards, the approach of "No to GMOs, Yes to Natural Worms!" presents a natural and healthy option. If necessary regulations are made in Turkey, mealworm farming could significantly contribute to food supply security.
